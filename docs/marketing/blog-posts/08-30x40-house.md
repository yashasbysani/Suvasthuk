The 30×40 site (1,200 sq ft) is the workhorse of Bengaluru residential construction — common across BBMP layouts, BDA sites, and private developments city-wide. Its dimensions are generous enough for a genuinely comfortable home and tight enough that design discipline matters at every stage. This is what 30 years of building on this exact footprint has taught us.

## How much of a 30×40 site can you actually build on?

Mandatory setbacks eat into the plot before design even begins. For a standard 30×40 residential plot in a BBMP layout, typical setback requirements are:

- **Front:** 3.0 m (10 ft) minimum, more on wider roads
- **Rear:** 1.8–2.4 m (6–8 ft)
- **Sides:** 1.5–1.8 m (5–6 ft) each

After setbacks, the buildable footprint on a 30×40 (30 ft × 40 ft) plot lands around **18–20 ft × 25–28 ft per floor** — roughly 450–560 sq ft of ground coverage, before FAR and coverage-ratio limits are applied on top. This is why professional design matters so much on this size: the difference between a cramped 30×40 and a genuinely spacious one is almost entirely how well the buildable rectangle is used, not the plot's raw dimensions.

There's no single "ideal" — it depends on family size and whether rental income matters — but the recurring patterns that work well:

- **Ground floor:** living + dining as one flowing space toward the front/garden, kitchen to the rear or side (often south-east for [Vastu](/blog/vastu-shastra-modern-homes)), one bedroom with attached toilet for elders, a small utility/wash area, and the staircase positioned to not eat into the living volume.
- **First floor:** 2–3 bedrooms with attached toilets, a family/TV area, balconies toward the front and/or rear for cross-ventilation.
- **Second floor (if G+2):** either an independent rental unit with its own entry via the staircase, or a master suite + terrace for the owner's family, depending on whether income or space is the priority.

The staircase location is the single highest-leverage design decision on a 30×40 — badly placed, it steals light and area from every floor it touches; well placed, it becomes a light-well and circulation spine at once.

These figures cover construction only — compound wall, borewell, BBMP approval, and interiors sit outside them (see the full cost guide for what to add).

G+2 usually makes better financial sense on this plot size, for three reasons: foundation and roof costs spread across more built-up area (lowering effective ₹/sq ft), an independent second-floor unit in most Bengaluru micro-markets rents for enough to meaningfully offset a home loan EMI, and FAR on most 30×40 BBMP sites comfortably permits it. The trade-off is a higher upfront outlay and, if renting, the practicalities of an independent-unit design (separate entrance, separate meter provision, sound insulation between floors).

Our recommendation on every 30×40 brief: design the full G+2 envelope from day one — structure, staircase, plumbing stacks — even if you plan to finish only G+1 initially. Retrofitting a second floor onto an unprepared structure costs far more than building it to spec the first time.

## Common design mistakes on 30×40 plots

- **Staircase placed centrally**, cutting every floor's usable width instead of running along one side.
- **Toilets clustered on the plot's dark core** with no ventilation shaft, relying entirely on exhaust fans.
- **No provision for a second-floor independent entrance**, discovered only when rental plans firm up after construction.
- **Full-width front setback wasted as bare concrete** instead of doubling as parking + a small landscaped buffer.
- **Structure not designed for the eventual G+2**, forcing expensive column/footing strengthening later.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many sq ft is a 30×40 site, and how much is buildable?**
A 30×40 plot is 1,200 sq ft of land. After mandatory setbacks (roughly 3 m front, 1.8–2.4 m rear, 1.5–1.8 m sides), the buildable footprint per floor is typically 450–560 sq ft, meaning a G+2 home can reach roughly 2,900–3,200 sq ft of total built-up area across three floors — well over double the plot area, which is the whole logic of building multiple floors on a compact urban site.

**Can I build 3 floors (G+2) on a 30×40 site in Bangalore?**
Generally yes, subject to your specific zone's FAR and height regulations and the road width your plot fronts — wider roads typically permit more. Confirm your site's exact FAR entitlement before finalizing the design; we check this as the first step of every [sanction plan](/services/sanction-plans-tdr) engagement, since it decides the whole design brief.

**What is a good rental income design for a 30×40 G+2?**
The most successful designs give the rental floor (usually the second) a fully independent entrance and staircase access, its own water and electrical sub-metering provision, at least one bedroom with attached toilet plus a compact kitchen, and sound-insulating floor detailing between levels. Planning this at design stage — not retrofitting after occupancy — is what makes it feel like a proper independent unit rather than a converted room.

**How long does it take to build a house on a 30×40 plot?**
For a G+2 with standard finishes, realistic Bengaluru timelines run **8–12 months** from foundation to handover, assuming approvals are already in hand and decisions (material selections, design sign-offs) are made on schedule. Monsoon timing and finalization delays are the two most common causes of overrun — we sequence major concreting work around the rains wherever the schedule allows.
